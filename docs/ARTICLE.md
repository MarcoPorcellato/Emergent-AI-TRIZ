# Do Large Language Models Rediscover TRIZ?

## The Latent TRIZ Hypothesis and a Public Experiment on the Internal Geometry of Inventive Thinking

**By Marco Porcellato**  
**Research proposal — August 2026**

---

## Introduction

For years, I treated TRIZ and artificial intelligence as two distinct parts of my professional life.

I trained as a mechanical engineer at the University of Padua and Politecnico di Milano, where my master’s thesis focused on methodologies supporting systematic innovation. I later studied TRIZ with Valeri Souchkov, obtained MATRIZ Level 2 certification, participated in the Italian TRIZ community, and worked in patent research and intellectual property before extending systematic innovation methods into strategy and marketing. [20]

Today, through Matryca.ai, I am developing local-first tools for personal knowledge management, agentic memory, and human–AI collaboration.

**Matryca Plumber** provides humans and AI agents with a safe, structured interface to a human-readable Logseq knowledge graph, while Markdown remains the authoritative source of truth. [18]

**Matryca Brain** explores a broader question: how can a knowledge system learn which information matters, reinforce useful ideas through human feedback, and evolve over time without surrendering human ownership of knowledge? [19]

While studying the internal representations of large language models, I began to see a deeper connection between my work on LLMs and the intellectual history of TRIZ.

That connection led me to formulate a hypothesis that is both ambitious and experimentally testable:

> **Large language models may independently develop internal representations functionally equivalent to some TRIZ Inventive Principles, because those principles describe recurring, domain-independent transformations of problems.**

I call this the **Latent TRIZ Hypothesis**.

The purpose of this article is not to claim that the hypothesis is already true. It is to define it precisely enough that it can be tested, challenged, falsified, and improved in public.

# From Patents to Inventive Principles

TRIZ did not begin as a collection of creativity prompts.

Genrikh Altshuller and his collaborators studied large bodies of patent literature and searched for recurring ways in which inventors resolved technical contradictions. Historical accounts disagree on the exact size of the corpus. AITRIZ reports that approximately 200,000 patent disclosures were examined and that roughly 40,000 breakthrough inventions were generalized. The Altshuller Institute describes the process more cautiously as the investigation and synthesis of thousands of patents. [1,2]

The exact number is less important than the intellectual operation that was performed:

\[
\text{many concrete inventions}
\rightarrow
\text{recurring transformations}
\rightarrow
\text{domain-independent principles}
\]

The original inventions belonged to different industries and solved different physical problems.

Altshuller’s achievement was to remove much of their specific content and preserve the reusable transformation.

For example, the principle of **Segmentation** can be expressed abstractly as:

\[
\text{one integrated object}
\rightarrow
\text{multiple coordinated parts}
\]

The principle of **Inversion** can be expressed as:

\[
\text{perform action } A
\rightarrow
\text{perform the opposite, reverse, or complementary action}
\]

The principle of **Dynamics** suggests:

\[
\text{fixed structure}
\rightarrow
\text{structure that adapts to changing conditions}
\]

The principle of **Feedback** can be expressed as:

\[
\text{open-loop action}
\rightarrow
\text{action modified by information about its effects}
\]

Seen from this perspective, the Inventive Principles resemble operators:

\[
T_k(P)=P'
\]

where:

- \(P\) is the original representation of a problem;
- \(T_k\) is an inventive transformation;
- \(P'\) is a new representation in which the contradiction may become solvable.

TRIZ made these transformations explicit, symbolic, teachable, and human-readable. [2,3]

The laboratory also maintains a provenance-bound expert reference layer: an
illustrated forty-principle teaching set by Robert Adunka, the 2003 update of
the contradiction matrix by Mann, Dewulf, Zlotin, and Zusman, and a conceptual
map of interactions among TRIZ tools attributed to Gregor Panitz. [21–23]
These sources broaden construct coverage and future test design. They are not
automatic ground truth and are kept separate from blinded tests of latent
rediscovery.

The question is whether neural networks may learn some of the same transformations in a completely different format: not as named rules, but as structures in activation space.

# The Parallel with Language-Model Training

A language model is formally trained to predict the next token.

However, predicting text across trillions of tokens requires more than memorizing isolated word sequences. Text is generated by underlying processes:

- objects interact;
- agents pursue goals;
- causes produce effects;
- systems operate under constraints;
- plans succeed or fail;
- contradictions arise;
- solutions transform an initial state into a more desirable one.

To predict text well, a model benefits from reconstructing some of these latent regularities.

This does not mean that the model necessarily represents them in the same way humans do. It means that an internal variable corresponding to some underlying regularity may reduce prediction error.

The training process can therefore be viewed as a vast compression problem:

\[
\text{human-generated text}
\rightarrow
\text{prediction pressure}
\rightarrow
\text{reusable internal representations}
\]

TRIZ and LLM training are not equivalent processes.

Altshuller and his collaborators consciously selected, interpreted, classified, criticized, and formalized inventions. Gradient descent has no conscious methodology and receives no explicit instruction to discover inventive principles.

Nevertheless, both processes face a related problem:

> How can a very large number of concrete examples be represented through a much smaller number of reusable regularities?

TRIZ answers this problem symbolically.

An LLM may answer it geometrically.

# Why Abstraction Could Emerge Naturally

There are several reasons why a language model might develop abstract representations of problem-solving transformations.

## Compression rewards reusable structure

Suppose a model encounters thousands of examples in which a monolithic system is replaced by independent modules.

Memorizing every example separately is possible, but inefficient.

A representation corresponding approximately to:

\[
\text{monolithic}
\rightarrow
\text{segmented}
\]

can be reused across software, engineering, biology, organizations, logistics, and communication.

Generalization is, in part, efficient compression.

## The architecture favors geometric representations

Transformers operate mainly through vectors, matrix multiplications, attention, nonlinear transformations, and residual connections.

A property represented as a direction or subspace can be:

- detected by a linear projection;
- combined with other properties;
- amplified;
- suppressed;
- copied between token positions;
- transformed by later layers.

A reusable concept encoded geometrically is therefore convenient for the machinery that must operate on it.

## Problem-solving procedures may be compositional

Inventive thinking may not require one monolithic “creativity module.”

It may arise from combinations of simpler operations:

\[
\text{detect conflict}
+
\text{change abstraction level}
+
\text{retrieve analogy}
+
\text{transform constraint}
+
\text{evaluate result}
\]

Different compositions of a limited set of operators could produce very different forms of reasoning.

This is conceptually similar to how a limited instruction set can support an enormous number of computer programs.

## Some apparent emergence may still be a measurement effect

The literature on emergent abilities requires caution.

Some studies report capabilities that appear suddenly at particular model scales. Other work shows that apparent discontinuities can be produced by nonlinear metrics or thresholds even when the underlying performance improves smoothly. [4,5]

The Latent TRIZ Hypothesis therefore does not depend on a dramatic, instantaneous “emergence moment.”

A representation may develop gradually while becoming behaviorally useful only after several supporting mechanisms become sufficiently reliable.

# The Latent TRIZ Hypothesis

I propose two versions of the hypothesis.

## The Weak Latent TRIZ Hypothesis

> A pretrained language model contains internal representations that correspond to at least some TRIZ Inventive Principles, and those representations generalize across substantially different domains.

Under this hypothesis, examples of Segmentation in mechanical engineering, software architecture, biology, logistics, and marketing would share some detectable internal structure.

This would be interesting, but it would not prove independent rediscovery.

A pretrained model may have encountered:

- TRIZ books;
- articles about the 40 Principles;
- patent-analysis material;
- training courses;
- examples explicitly labelled with TRIZ terminology.

The model might therefore be retrieving or reconstructing learned descriptions of TRIZ.

## The Strong Latent TRIZ Hypothesis

> A neural sequence model can develop internal representations functionally equivalent to TRIZ Inventive Principles by learning from unlabelled problem–solution examples, without receiving principle names, TRIZ definitions, canonical examples, or explicit TRIZ instruction.

This is the stronger claim.

It proposes that some inventive transformations emerge because they are useful for representing problem solving—not merely because the model has read about TRIZ.

The weak hypothesis can be investigated using existing open models.

The strong hypothesis requires a controlled training experiment.

---

# What Would Count as a Latent Inventive Principle?

A cluster in a PCA visualization would not be sufficient.

A model correctly defining Segmentation would not be sufficient.

A probe classifying examples with high accuracy would still not be sufficient.

To call something a latent inventive principle, I would require three progressively stronger properties.

## 1. Cross-domain decodability

There must be an internal representation from which the principle can be predicted across domains.

For example, a probe trained on:

- mechanical examples;
- software examples;
- biological examples;

should identify Segmentation in held-out organizational or marketing examples.

If the probe works only within one domain, it may be detecting subject matter rather than an abstract transformation.

## 2. Lexical independence

The representation must survive the removal of obvious linguistic clues.

Examples of Segmentation should not all contain words such as:

- divide;
- split;
- modular;
- parts;
- separate.

The experiment must include paraphrases, adversarial examples, and near-miss cases designed to break superficial word associations.

## 3. Causal influence

Manipulating the representation must change the model’s problem-solving behavior in a principle-specific way.

If a candidate representation for Segmentation is called:

\[
v_{\text{segmentation}}
\]

and the model’s normal activation at a selected layer is:

\[
h
\]

we can intervene with:

\[
h'=h+\alpha v_{\text{segmentation}}
\]

where \(\alpha\) controls the strength of the intervention.

The decisive question is not whether the model becomes more likely to say the word *segmentation*.

The question is:

> Does the model become more likely to generate genuinely segmented solutions to previously unseen contradictions?

The opposite intervention should also be tested:

\[
h'=h-\alpha v_{\text{segmentation}}
\]

or by projecting the candidate component out of the activation.

If positive steering increases principle-consistent solutions and ablation reduces them, the representation is no longer merely correlated with the principle. It is participating in the computation that produces that class of solution.

# Why This Is Experimentally Possible Now

Mechanistic interpretability has begun to provide the necessary tools.

## Emergent internal state representations

In the Othello-GPT experiment, a Transformer trained only to predict legal moves developed an internal representation of the game board despite never receiving an explicit board representation.

The researchers did not stop at decoding the board state. They intervened on the internal representation and changed the model’s move predictions consistently with a counterfactual board configuration. [6]

This matters because it demonstrates that next-token prediction can create a causally active representation of an underlying process.

## Representation Engineering

Representation Engineering studies high-level properties through population-level activation patterns rather than individual neurons.

It provides methods for reading and manipulating concepts and behaviors represented in activation space. [7]

## Contrastive Activation Addition

Contrastive Activation Addition creates steering vectors from differences between activations generated by positive and negative examples.

A simplified version is:

\[
v=
\operatorname{mean}(h_{\text{positive}})
-
\operatorname{mean}(h_{\text{negative}})
\]

The resulting vector can be added during inference to influence model behavior. [8]

For Project Latent TRIZ, this offers an immediate way to construct candidate principle directions.

## The Jacobian Lens and J-space

In July 2026, Anthropic introduced the Jacobian Lens and reported evidence for a collection of verbalizable internal representations called the **J-space**.

The Jacobian Lens estimates how a perturbation at an intermediate layer affects what the model may verbalize later. Anthropic reports that some of these representations can be reported, deliberately activated, used in intermediate reasoning, transferred between contexts, and manipulated causally. [9]

This does not provide a literal or complete reader of model thoughts.

The authors explicitly describe the lens as incomplete, approximate, and currently biased toward concepts that correspond to individual vocabulary tokens.

Nevertheless, it offers a principled method for investigating whether a model has internally made a concept available to downstream computation.

Anthropic has also released reference code for fitting and applying Jacobian lenses to open-weight decoder models. [10]

# Project Latent TRIZ

I propose developing the experiment publicly under the working name **Project Latent TRIZ**.

The project should contain two complementary research tracks.

---

## Track A: Inspect Existing Open Models

The first track asks:

> Do pretrained open models already contain domain-general representations corresponding to TRIZ principles?

The initial test model would be **Gemma 3 270M**.

Google reports that the 270M model was trained on approximately six trillion tokens. It is small enough to inspect, modify, and repeatedly execute on consumer hardware, while still being exposed to a very large and diverse corpus. [11]

It also has a major practical advantage: Google DeepMind released Gemma Scope 2, which includes sparse autoencoders and transcoders covering every layer of Gemma 3 models from 270M to 27B parameters. [12]

The initial model sequence could be:

\[
\text{Gemma 3 270M}
\rightarrow
\text{Gemma 3 1B}
\rightarrow
\text{Gemma 3 4B}
\rightarrow
\text{Qwen and other families}
\]

The 270M model is not expected to be the final judge of the hypothesis.

It is the microscope slide.

Its small size allows rapid iteration on:

- activation collection;
- layer-by-layer probing;
- sparse-feature analysis;
- steering;
- ablation;
- causal tracing;
- experimental controls.

A negative result on 270M would not falsify the general hypothesis. The model may simply be too limited to learn robust cross-domain inventive operators.

The experiment must therefore study how the representations change with model scale.

---

## Track B: Controlled Emergence from Scratch

The second track addresses training-data contamination.

A small Transformer would be trained from scratch on a synthetic or carefully curated corpus containing:

- problems;
- constraints;
- contradictions;
- transformations;
- outcomes.

The corpus would contain recurring inventive structures but no:

- TRIZ terminology;
- principle numbers;
- principle names;
- contradiction matrix;
- canonical textbook examples;
- explanations of TRIZ.

For example, the model could encounter many independent cases in which a fixed integrated system is replaced by coordinated units.

These cases would appear in unrelated synthetic domains and use different vocabulary.

Some domains would be withheld entirely during training.

The model would then be tested on new problems in those held-out domains.

This track asks:

> If the same transformation repeatedly helps predict successful problem–solution sequences, does the model develop a shared internal representation of that transformation?

This would be similar in scientific spirit to Othello-GPT: the full data-generating process would be known, and the model could not have copied the target abstraction from an external TRIZ source. [6]

---

# Stage 1: Build a Cross-Domain Dataset

Starting with all 40 principles would be a mistake.

Several principles overlap, many solutions instantiate multiple principles, and some principles are strongly tied to physical engineering.

The first study should focus on a smaller set of relatively distinguishable transformations, such as:

1. Segmentation  
2. Taking Out  
3. Local Quality  
4. Inversion  
5. Dynamics  
6. Another Dimension  
7. Feedback  
8. Intermediary  

For each principle, the dataset should include problem–solution pairs from multiple domains:

- mechanical engineering;
- software;
- logistics;
- biology;
- organizational design;
- product design;
- marketing;
- everyday problem solving.

Each item should contain:

- the initial state;
- the desired improvement;
- the worsening consequence or contradiction;
- the transformation applied;
- the resulting state;
- expert principle labels;
- alternative valid labels;
- a confidence score;
- near-miss solutions;
- lexically similar but conceptually different controls.

TRIZ classification is frequently multi-label.

The dataset should therefore allow several principles to be assigned to one solution rather than forcing every example into exactly one category.

Independent TRIZ experts should annotate the examples without knowing which model condition will later be evaluated.

---

# Stage 2: Establish Surface-Level Baselines

Before studying internal activations, we must establish how much of the task can be solved superficially.

Baselines should include:

- bag-of-words classification;
- conventional sentence embeddings;
- topic classification;
- keyword matching;
- output-only LLM classification;
- random-label controls.

If a simple text classifier already identifies the principle across domains, the dataset may contain obvious lexical leakage.

The dataset should be redesigned until successful classification requires understanding the transformation rather than recognizing recurring words.

---

# Stage 3: Map Internal Representations

For each problem and solution, residual-stream activations would be collected across layers.

The analysis could include:

- linear probes;
- nonlinear probes;
- contrastive difference-of-means vectors;
- representational similarity analysis;
- principal component analysis;
- sparse autoencoder features;
- Gemma Scope 2 transcoders;
- activation patching;
- causal tracing;
- logit-lens readouts;
- Jacobian-lens readouts.

Sparse autoencoders are particularly useful because dense model activations may contain many overlapping features.

An SAE attempts to reconstruct a dense activation through a larger but sparse set of features, making it easier to search for recurring internal components.

Gemma Scope established this approach for Gemma 2, while Gemma Scope 2 extends the tooling across Gemma 3 model sizes and layers. [12,13]

The key evaluation would be **leave-one-domain-out generalization**.

A principle representation discovered using several domains must still work in a domain that was not used to discover it.

---

# Stage 4: Perform Causal Steering

Candidate principle representations could come from several sources:

- contrastive activation differences;
- trained probe directions;
- SAE decoder features;
- Jacobian-lens vectors;
- combinations of sparse features.

Each candidate would be tested through controlled interventions.

For a target principle \(k\):

\[
h'=h+\alpha v_k
\]

The coefficient \(\alpha\) would be varied across positive and negative values.

Controls should include:

- a random vector with the same norm;
- an unrelated principle vector;
- a semantically related but non-inventive vector;
- a style or verbosity vector;
- activation replacement from another example;
- no intervention.

Outputs would be evaluated blindly by TRIZ experts.

The judges would assess:

- whether the proposed solution resolves the contradiction;
- which principles it instantiates;
- whether the target principle became more prevalent;
- feasibility;
- originality;
- constraint adherence;
- whether the answer merely repeated principle-related vocabulary.

A valid steering effect must change the **solution strategy**, not just the words used to describe it.

---

# Stage 5: Test Composition

TRIZ principles are rarely isolated in real inventions.

This creates a stronger test.

Suppose the experiment identifies:

\[
v_{\text{segmentation}}
\]

and:

\[
v_{\text{dynamics}}
\]

We can test a combined intervention:

\[
h'
=
h
+
\alpha v_{\text{segmentation}}
+
\beta v_{\text{dynamics}}
\]

Does the model become more likely to generate a solution that is both modular and dynamically adaptable?

If two principle representations compose in a predictable way, they begin to resemble an algebra of transformations rather than independent behavioral associations.

The experiment may also show that traditional principles are not represented as clean, orthogonal directions.

They may instead exist as:

- overlapping subspaces;
- sparse feature combinations;
- nonlinear manifolds;
- context-dependent circuits;
- families of related transformations.

That would not necessarily falsify the Latent TRIZ Hypothesis.

It might reveal that the 40 Principles are a useful human-readable projection of a richer computational structure.

---

# Stage 6: Replicate Across Models

Any convincing result must replicate.

A candidate latent principle should ideally recur across:

- multiple problem domains;
- base and instruction-tuned models;
- different model sizes;
- different model families;
- independently generated test sets;
- different prompts;
- different feature-discovery methods;
- different groups of TRIZ evaluators.

The geometry does not need to be numerically identical across models.

Different models can encode the same function in different coordinates.

The important question is whether they develop functionally equivalent representations that support similar cross-domain generalization and causal intervention.

---

# What Would Count as Strong Evidence?

The strongest evidence would combine the following results.

## Cross-domain representation

The target principle can be decoded from internal activations in domains not used to discover it.

## Lexical robustness

The result survives paraphrasing, synonym removal, adversarial wording, and topic controls.

## Causal specificity

Steering selectively increases solutions using the target principle rather than merely increasing creativity, compliance, confidence, or verbosity.

## Bidirectional control

Positive steering increases target-principle solutions, while ablation or negative steering reduces them.

## Novel-problem generalization

The effect appears on contradictions created after the representation-discovery process is complete.

## Compositionality

Two or more principle representations can be combined to produce predictable hybrid solution strategies.

## Cross-model recurrence

Functionally comparable representations appear in independently trained model families.

## Controlled emergence

A model trained without TRIZ language develops equivalent operators from recurring unlabelled problem–solution patterns.

The final result would be the most important.

It would make direct memorization of TRIZ descriptions an inadequate explanation.

---

# What Would Weaken or Falsify the Hypothesis?

Project Latent TRIZ must be designed to produce meaningful negative results.

The hypothesis would be weakened if:

- principle classification disappears after lexical controls;
- probes fail on held-out domains;
- steering changes vocabulary but not strategy;
- every principle vector produces the same generic “creative” behavior;
- interventions mainly damage fluency;
- effects appear only after explicit TRIZ prompting;
- no corresponding ablation effect is found;
- representations fail to replicate on newly generated problems;
- effects do not recur in other model families;
- controlled models fail to develop shared transformation structures.

It is also possible that only some principles emerge robustly.

That would be scientifically valuable.

Some TRIZ principles may reflect highly general cognitive transformations, while others may be practical engineering heuristics linked to particular physical technologies.

The experiment could therefore teach us something about TRIZ itself.

---

# How This Differs from Existing LLM–TRIZ Research

Several projects already use LLMs to apply or automate TRIZ.

AutoTRIZ uses language models to automate stages of TRIZ-based engineering ideation. [14]

TRIZ Agents explores a multi-agent architecture in which specialized agents collaborate through a TRIZ-oriented process. [15]

TRIZ-RAGNER uses retrieval-augmented language models to identify TRIZ contradiction parameters in patent text. [16]

These are important directions, but they generally ask:

> Can an LLM apply TRIZ when TRIZ knowledge, workflows, retrieval systems, labels, or instructions are supplied?

Project Latent TRIZ asks something different:

> Has the model already learned internal abstractions functionally equivalent to parts of TRIZ before we explicitly tell it to use TRIZ?

This reverses the usual relationship.

TRIZ becomes not only a methodology given to the model, but also a scientific instrument for investigating what the model has abstracted from human problem solving.

---

# Would This Disprove the “Stochastic Parrot” View?

The phrase *stochastic parrot* comes from the influential paper by Bender, Gebru, McMillan-Major, and Shmitchell. That paper raised broad concerns involving training-data documentation, bias, environmental and financial costs, linguistic form, scale, and the social interpretation of apparently coherent model output. [17]

A successful Latent TRIZ experiment would not refute those broader concerns.

It would not prove:

- consciousness;
- subjective experience;
- human-equivalent understanding;
- complete grounding in the physical world;
- moral agency;
- reliability;
- freedom from bias or hallucination.

It would also be scientifically irresponsible to describe one experiment as an unequivocal proof that an LLM “understands the world” in every meaningful sense.

The narrower conclusion could still be extremely important.

Suppose an unlabelled, cross-domain inventive operator could be:

- decoded;
- transferred to unseen domains;
- injected;
- removed;
- combined with other operators;
- shown to redirect novel problem solving;
- reproduced in a model never exposed to TRIZ terminology.

That would provide strong causal evidence against a strictly surface-level account in which the model only recombines local linguistic patterns without learning reusable abstract structure.

The critical distinction is:

> Language models are stochastic, but stochastic optimization can still produce structured internal models.

Stochasticity and representation are not opposites.

Othello-GPT is a controlled example: next-token prediction produced a causally active representation of an underlying game state. [6]

Project Latent TRIZ would investigate whether next-token prediction can also produce representations of **abstract problem transformations**.

That is a more ambitious target.

---

# What This Could Teach Us About TRIZ

The experiment is not only a test of language models.

It could become a new way to study the internal structure of TRIZ.

We may discover that:

- some principles form stable cross-domain representations;
- some traditional principles collapse into one shared latent operator;
- some principles are combinations of more primitive transformations;
- some are strongly context-dependent;
- some cannot be separated computationally;
- models contain recurring inventive operators not clearly represented in the traditional 40 Principles.

This suggests a larger possibility:

> TRIZ may be a human-readable approximation of a broader latent algebra of inventive transformation.

Altshuller and his collaborators discovered regularities by studying the products of human invention.

Language models may discover related regularities by compressing a vast amount of recorded human thought.

Both processes may be responding to structures imposed by the problems themselves:

- systems contain components;
- resources are limited;
- properties conflict;
- actions produce side effects;
- constraints can be separated in space, time, scale, or condition;
- successful transformations recur across domains.

Perhaps some Inventive Principles are not only conventions developed within TRIZ.

Perhaps they are natural coordinates of problem transformation.

---

# Why This Matters for Matryca

Matryca Plumber began with the architecture of memory:

> How can humans and agents safely maintain the same durable body of knowledge while the human remains the owner of that knowledge? [18]

Matryca Brain extends the question:

> How can a knowledge system learn salience, reinforce useful ideas, respond to human feedback, and evolve over time? [19]

But memory and salience alone are not sufficient for intelligence.

A cognitive system also needs reusable operations for transforming knowledge:

- detecting contradictions;
- reframing problems;
- changing abstraction levels;
- transferring solutions across domains;
- generating alternatives;
- evaluating transformations;
- learning which transformations succeed.

TRIZ offers an explicit human framework for some of these operations.

Mechanistic interpretability may reveal whether comparable operations already exist inside language models.

The three research directions can therefore be understood as complementary:

> **Matryca Plumber investigates shared memory.**

> **Matryca Brain investigates adaptive knowledge and salience.**

> **Project Latent TRIZ investigates the operators of inventive cognition.**

# An Invitation to Collaborate

I am proposing Project Latent TRIZ as an open research initiative.

It requires several kinds of expertise.

TRIZ practitioners are needed to:

- select the initial principles;
- design valid contradictions;
- classify ambiguous examples;
- distinguish real principle application from superficial terminology;
- evaluate generated solutions.

Mechanistic interpretability researchers are needed to:

- design probes;
- identify activation directions;
- analyze sparse features;
- fit Jacobian lenses;
- perform causal interventions;
- create appropriate controls.

Machine-learning researchers are needed to:

- build the controlled training environment;
- generate synthetic domains;
- study scaling behavior;
- replicate findings across architectures.

Patent and innovation specialists are needed to:

- contribute technically credible examples;
- distinguish routine improvements from inventive transformations;
- connect the experiment to the historical foundations of TRIZ.

Cognitive scientists and philosophers of AI are needed to:

- clarify what the evidence does and does not demonstrate;
- prevent causal representation from being confused with consciousness;
- develop precise interpretations of abstraction, understanding, and world modelling.

The protocol, code, datasets, annotations, evaluation criteria, results, and failures should be public.

The project should begin not by assuming that the hypothesis is true, but by asking:

> What experiment would genuinely distinguish a domain-general inventive operator from memorization, lexical similarity, prompting effects, evaluator expectations, or generic increases in creativity?

That is the standard Project Latent TRIZ must meet.

---

# Conclusion

Altshuller asked whether invention contains recurring patterns.

Modern interpretability research asks whether intelligent-looking behavior is supported by structured and causally active internal representations.

Project Latent TRIZ places these two questions on the same experimental bench.

Perhaps language models only reproduce TRIZ patterns encountered during training.

Perhaps the 40 Inventive Principles are useful human heuristics but do not correspond to stable internal structures.

Or perhaps Altshuller and his collaborators extracted a visible fragment of something deeper: a compact family of transformations toward which both human analysis and machine learning can converge when exposed to enough examples of successful invention.

If so, TRIZ may be more than a methodology for prompting creative thought.

It may be a partial symbolic map of the geometry of inventive intelligence.

That is not yet a conclusion.

It is now a falsifiable hypothesis.

And I believe it is worth testing in public.
